                  Your rules

                  Your rules are checked first, then the built-in protections.

                  What the words mean
                  Masked
                  Before the AI saw it, Mandu replaced the real text with a short code that stands for it. You still see the real text.
                  No AI
                  Every AI sees it masked, Mandu's helper included, and your OK does not change that. Only the programs allowed to open it get the real file: ssh, and programs you name, never a database shell. It is sent only where a rule or your OK allows.
                  Your own AI only
                  Only your own AI may read it, when it asks: one you or your organization run, on this computer or your organization's network, listed in Settings › Models. Any other AI sees it masked, and your OK does not change that. It is sent only where a rule or your OK allows.
                  Any AI, when it asks
                  The AI first sees it masked. When it needs the contents, Mandu's helper reads them for it, or the AI reads them itself. Each read is logged, and your AI provider gets what is read. It is sent only where a rule or your OK allows.
                  Not secret
                  Treated like any other file, even if it looks like a secret (a test .env, for example).
                  Trusted source
                  What comes from it reaches the AI unmasked.
                  Always allowed
                  Happens without asking you.
                  Always blocked
                  Stopped without asking you.
                  Needs your OK
                  The default: Mandu pauses and asks you first. One exception: a key whose shape names the company that issued it, such as a Stripe or GitHub key, goes to that company's own API without asking (issuer_bindings in policy.yaml turns this off).
                  Listed hosts without asking
                  For a secret: the hosts listed on its rule get the real value without asking you, over HTTPS too. Any other send still needs a rule or your OK. A program that read two secrets may send only where both may go.
                  Per value
                  Shown, greyed, in the second menu of a file that holds several secrets, such as a .env, when each value has its own listed hosts. Those are set in policy.yaml. A program that opened the whole file may send it only where every value may go.
                  Your answer
                  Your choice when Mandu asked and you picked always. For sending, it covers only the data you were asked about, going to that one place. Other data sent there still needs your OK.

                              Models

                              Which models may read files marked "Your own AI only".

                              More

                              A model you do not mark as your own is an outside model. Changes are saved to models: in policy.yaml.

                              It runs on this computer, or on a server your organization controls. On another computer, it must use an https address.
                              Only for your own model at an http address on another computer, on a network you trust. Files marked "Your own AI only" then cross the network unencrypted. Prefer an https address.
                              When you list more than one model of your own, files marked "Your own AI only" are read by this one. Without a choice, the first model of your own in the list reads them.

                              Sensitivity labels

                              Give your organization's document labels (like "Highly Confidential") a level.

                              More

                              Your organization's Office puts these labels on documents. Mandu's scan suggests a label's level for every document that carries it, and nothing is protected until you save a suggestion. Saved to sensitivity_labels: in policy.yaml.
